Which thread is on which key

Keys run 1 and 2 on the top row, then 3 to 6 on the row below, left to right. The bridge fills them by urgency: waiting on you first (longest wait first), then failed, then finished, then your own running threads, then scheduled ones, then idle threads with pinned ones ahead.

So you read the pad by color, from key 1. Threads come from T3 Code on this Mac, and from any other T3 server you list in t3Homes. Settled and snoozed threads never take a key.

Prefer the order of the sidebar? Set "slotOrder": "sidebar" in ~/Library/Application Support/T3MicroBridge/config.json and restart the bridge.

The ring around the pad

It watches every open thread, including ones that did not fit on the six keys.

  • Orange pulse: something waits on you. Press NEXT.
  • Blue, moving: work is running, nothing needs you.
  • Red or green, steady: a failure or a finished thread to look at.
  • A short green blink after NEXT: nothing is waiting.
  • White or violet for a second: you switched to simple or advanced.

Learn it in four sittings

  1. Read, do not press

    Leave the pad on its T3 Code layer and work as usual. Glance at it when a thread finishes or asks something. Orange and green are the only colors that want you.

  2. NEXT and the six keys

    When the ring pulses orange, press NEXT. Press a lit key to open that thread. Turn the knob to step through threads.

  3. MIC, SEND, SETTLE

    Hold MIC and talk, let go, press SEND. When you are done with a thread, press SETTLE and the next thread moves up.

  4. Advanced

    Hold the knob until the ring turns violet. Now hold a thread key to settle it without opening the menu, and use the joystick for back, forward, effort and sidebar.

Why the keys are where they are

The layout follows how people work with several agent threads at once.

NEXTthe thread that waits on you

Agents stop to ask questions, and a question you miss holds its thread for as long as you miss it.

One key opens the longest wait. The ring pulses orange for any waiting thread, not only the six on the keys.

APPROVEand decline on a hold

With agents in auto or full access, approval requests are rare.

Approve has one key. Decline is a hold on it, so a slip does not refuse a request.

SETTLEclear a finished thread

Settling is the main way to keep the thread list short.

SETTLE has its own key. In advanced mode, holding a thread key settles that thread.

STOPend a run

A run that went the wrong way should stop at once.

STOP has a key. T3 Code has no shortcut for it, so setup adds one.

ORDERby urgency, not position

With several threads running, the top of the sidebar is often pinned and idle.

Keys follow urgency, and threads you started rank ahead of scheduled ones.

MICtalk, then SEND

A short prompt is faster to say than to type.

Hold MIC and talk, let go, press SEND.
